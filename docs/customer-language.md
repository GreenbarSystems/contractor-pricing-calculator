# Customer language guide

The calculator is for contractors who want a dependable answer to one question: **What should I charge?** It must not require accounting training.

## Use these words

| Calculator wording | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| Labor pay | What you expect to pay your crew for this job. |
| Extra cost on top of wages | Employer costs such as payroll taxes, workers' comp, paid time off, benefits, and similar costs. |
| Your material cost | What you expect to pay suppliers. |
| Other job costs | Permits, rentals, subcontractors, delivery, disposal, and other job-specific costs. |
| Business costs for this job | The job's share of the costs of running the business: trucks, insurance, software, office, advertising, and similar costs. |
| Hours you expect to sell this year | Customer work hours you expect to charge for, not every paid hour. |
| Lowest price that covers these costs | The modeled price floor before profit. |
| Recommended price to charge | The price that covers entered costs and reaches the selected profit goal. |
| Profit you want to keep | The amount left from the customer payment after the entered job costs are paid. |

## Do not lead with these terms

Avoid using these as primary labels: direct labor, labor burden, overhead allocation, absorption, contribution margin, gross margin, target selling price, cost of goods sold, or break-even analysis.

Technical terms may appear in optional learning content only when paired with the approved plain-language label.

## Result language

- Below cost: "This price does not cover the costs you entered."
- Below goal: "This price covers your costs but misses your profit goal."
- Meets goal: "This price meets or beats your profit goal."

## Required disclosure

> This is a pricing estimate based on the numbers you entered. It helps you price a job, but it does not replace your accounting records or professional advice.
